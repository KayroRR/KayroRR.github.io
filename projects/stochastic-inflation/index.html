1. Problem Statement & Hamiltonian Stochastic Formalism

During cosmic inflation, vacuum quantum fluctuations of the scalar inflaton field $\phi$ are stretched beyond the Hubble radius $R_H = c/H$ ($H = \dot{a}/a$). Upon crossing the coarse-graining horizon scale $k_\sigma = \sigma a H$, non-commuting quantum operators undergo decoherence into two-mode squeezed states, acting as classical stochastic noise on long-wavelength super-Hubble modes ($\phi = \bar{\phi} + \phi_Q$, $\pi_\phi = \bar{\pi} + \pi_Q$).

In conformal time $\tau$, the coupled Hamiltonian dynamics of the coarse-grained phase-space vector $\Phi = (\bar{\phi}, \bar{\pi})^T$ is governed by the matrix Langevin equation:

$$\dot{\Phi} = A(\tau)\Phi + \xi(\tau), \qquad A(\tau) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & N/a^3 \\ -m^2 N a^3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \xi(\tau) = \begin{pmatrix} \xi_\phi(\tau) \\ \xi_\pi(\tau) \end{pmatrix}$$

where the quantum noise integrals are filtered by the window-function derivative $\dot{W}(k/k_\sigma)$—peaking sharply at the horizon-crossing threshold $k_\sigma$—and evaluated using conformal de Sitter mode functions $\phi_k(\tau) = \frac{1}{a\sqrt{2k}}\left(1 + \frac{i}{k\tau}\right)e^{ik\tau}$ and $\pi_k(\tau) = \sqrt{\frac{k}{2}}\,i\,e^{ik\tau}$.

Correspondingly, the joint probability density function $P(\Phi, \tau)$ satisfies the two-dimensional Fokker–Planck partial differential equation combining deterministic drift with quantum diffusion:

$$\frac{\partial P(\Phi,\tau)}{\partial \tau} = -\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial \Phi_i}\!\left[A_{ij}\Phi_j P(\Phi,\tau)\right] + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2} D_{ij}(\tau)\frac{\partial^2 P(\Phi,\tau)}{\partial \Phi_i \partial \Phi_j}$$

where the diffusion matrix $D_{ij}(\tau) = \frac{1}{2}(\Xi_{\phi\phi} + \Xi_{\pi\pi})I + \frac{1}{2}(\Xi_{\phi\pi} + \Xi_{\pi\phi})J_x + \frac{1}{2}(\Xi_{\phi\phi} - \Xi_{\pi\pi})J_z$ is constructed directly from the two-point quantum noise correlation matrix $\Xi(\vec{x}_1,\tau_1; \vec{x}_2,\tau_2)$ and Pauli matrices $J_i$.

2. Numerical Methodology

  • Monte Carlo Quantum Noise Sampling: Modeled creation and annihilation operators ($a_{\vec{k}}, a_{\vec{k}}^\dagger$) in 3D Fourier space as complex Gaussian random variables sampled via Monte Carlo to evaluate stochastic noise realizations $\xi_\phi(\tau), \xi_\pi(\tau)$ and expectation values of the correlation matrix $\Xi$.
  • Euler–Maruyama Langevin Ensembles: Integrated large ensembles of coupled SDE trajectories across Monte Carlo-perturbed initial conditions ("parallel universe realizations") to construct empirical marginal and joint PDFs of $\phi$ and $\pi$.
  • Finite-Difference Fokker–Planck Solver: Evolved $P(\phi, \pi, \tau)$ directly on a discretized 2D phase-space grid using the numerically averaged diffusion matrix $D_{ij}(\tau)$ to benchmark stochastic trajectories against deterministic PDE evolution.

3. Key Results & Cosmological Scope

  • Dynamical Horizon Freeze-Out: Spatial snapshots from $\tau = 0$ through intermediate ($\tau = \tau_{22}$) and late conformal times ($\tau = \tau_{41}$) confirm that sub-Hubble fluctuations decay and freeze out upon horizon exit, driving the field into a homogeneous slow-roll attractor.
  • Gaussian Slow-Roll Statistics: Independent stochastic realizations reproduce well-defined Gaussian distributions for both $\phi$ and $\pi$ with small momentum variance, consistent with Central Limit Theorem noise aggregation and standard slow-roll CMB predictions.
  • Phase-Space Coupling & Three Dynamical Regimes: 2D Fokker–Planck probability maps reveal diagonal correlation fringes evidencing coupled $(\phi, \pi)$ evolution during horizon crossing. Tracking statistical moments across $\tau \in [-20.0, 0.0]$ resolves three distinct stages: an initial-condition-dominated regime, a transient regime with strong correlations and non-Gaussianity, and a quasi-stationary regime stabilized around zero.
