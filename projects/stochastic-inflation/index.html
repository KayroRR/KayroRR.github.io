1. Theoretical Framework

During cosmic inflation, sub-Hubble quantum vacuum fluctuations are continuously stretched beyond the Hubble radius $H^{-1}$, undergoing decoherence and acting as a classical stochastic backreaction on the coarse-grained super-Hubble inflaton field $\bar{\phi}(N)$, parametrized by the number of $e$-folds $N = \ln a$:

$$\frac{d\bar{\phi}}{dN} = -\frac{V'(\bar{\phi})}{3H^2(\bar{\phi})} + \frac{H(\bar{\phi})}{2\pi}\xi(N), \qquad \langle \xi(N)\xi(N') \rangle = \delta(N - N')$$

Equivalently, the one-point probability density function $P(\phi, N)$ of the coarse-grained field satisfies the Fokker–Planck equation, enabling non-perturbative studies of quantum diffusion, heavy non-Gaussian tails, and primordial black hole formation thresholds beyond standard slow-roll perturbation theory.

2. Numerical Implementation

  • Stochastic Trajectory Ensembles: Implemented vectorized Euler–Maruyama solvers in Python to simulate $10^5$ independent Langevin realizations in phase space $(\bar{\phi}, \bar{\pi}_\phi)$.
  • Fokker–Planck Solver: Built a finite-difference PDE integrator for $P(\phi, N)$ with absorbing boundary conditions at the end-of-inflation surface to compute first-passage time distributions ($\mathcal{N}$-fold statistics).
  • Analytical Validation: Benchmarked numerical histograms and Fokker–Planck densities against exact Gaussian slow-roll stationary distributions in quadratic and plateau potentials.
