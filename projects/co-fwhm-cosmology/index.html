1. Research Question & Motivation

Dusty star-forming galaxies (DSFGs) and their submillimeter-selected counterparts (SMGs, $L_{\text{IR}} > 10^{12} L_\odot$) exhibit a strongly negative K-correction along the Rayleigh–Jeans dust tail ($S_\nu \propto \nu^{2+\beta}$), rendering their observed submillimeter flux nearly independent of redshift for $z \ge 1$. Moreover, flux-limited selections ($S_{500\,\mu\text{m}} > 100\text{ mJy}$) efficiently isolate strongly gravitationally lensed systems ($\mu \sim 5\text{--}30$).

While gravitational lensing amplifies integrated molecular line flux by the magnification factor $\mu = |\det \mathcal{A}|^{-1} = [(1-\kappa)^2 - \gamma^2]^{-1}$, the spectral Full Width at Half Maximum ($\text{FWHM}$) traces intrinsic Doppler kinematics within the galaxy's gravitational potential and remains strictly invariant under achromatic photon deflection. This study investigates how calibrating the empirical $L'_{\text{CO}(1-0)}\text{--FWHM}$ relation against lensing and merger systematics in control sources like HARPAS-51 enables high-redshift DSFGs to serve as standardizable cosmological candles.

2. Theoretical Framework & Standardization Formalism

Following Wu et al. (2019), restricting the correlation to the fundamental rotational transition $\text{CO}(J=1\text{--}0)$ eliminates excitation-ladder scatter and yields a redshift-invariant log-linear scaling relation between uncorrected line width $W_{\text{CO}}^{\text{uncorr}}$ and intrinsic CO luminosity:

$$\log_{10}\!\left(W_{\text{CO}}^{\text{uncorr}}\right) - A = \alpha + \beta\left[\log_{10}\!\left(L_{\text{CO}(1-0)}^{\prime\,\text{intrins}}\right) - B\right]$$

with pivot constants $A = 2.45$, $B = 9.81$, and locally calibrated zero-evolution parameters $\alpha = 0.03 \pm 0.05$, $\beta = 0.21 \pm 0.04$ ($\epsilon_i = 0.21$). Combining the inferred intrinsic luminosity with the observed velocity-integrated flux $I_{\text{CO}}$ ($\text{Jy km s}^{-1}$) and rest frequency $\nu_0$ ($\text{GHz}$) yields cosmology-independent luminosity distances $D_L(z)$:

$$L_{\text{CO}(1-0)}^{\prime\,\text{intrins}} = \frac{L'_{\text{CO}(1-0)}}{\mu} = \frac{3.25 \times 10^7\, I_{\text{CO}}\, D_L^2(z)}{\mu\,\nu_0^2(1+z)}, \qquad \text{DM} = 5\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{D_L}{\text{Mpc}}\right) + 25$$

Incorporating these high-redshift distance moduli ($z \sim 1\text{--}6.5$) into the Hubble diagram alongside Type Ia Supernovae tests extensions beyond $\Lambda\text{CDM}$, including constant-$w$ models ($w\text{CDM}$) and the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) dynamical dark energy parametrization:

$$w(a) = w_0 + w_a(1 - a) = w_0 + w_a\frac{z}{1+z}$$

directly probing parameter regions ($w_0 > -1, w_a < 0$) highlighted by recent DESI DR2 BAO, CMB, and supernova joint analyses.

3. Interferometric & Statistical Methodology

  • ALMA Image-Plane Reconstruction (CASA): Reduced archival ALMA interferometric visibilities for control source HARPAS-51 (selected from the Potential Lenses and Unidentified Mergers, PLUM-Fields sample) using the tclean deconvolution algorithm in CASA.
  • Spectral Line & Continuum Modeling (CARTA): Extracted integrated 1D spectra in CARTA and performed channel-rms-weighted Gaussian profile fits to measure $\nu_{\text{obs}}$, $z_{\text{spec}}$, $I_{\text{CO}}$, and $W_{\text{CO}}^{\text{uncorr}}$. Derived apparent molecular gas mass ($\mu M_{\text{H}_2} = \alpha_{\text{CO}} L'_{\text{CO}(1-0)}$) via DSFG excitation factors $r_{J,1}$, alongside dust mass ($M_{\text{dust}}$), $L_{\text{IR}}$, and star formation rate (SFR) via optically thin modified blackbody fits ($S_\nu \propto \nu^\beta B_\nu(T_{\text{dust}})$).
  • Systematics Calibration & Population Scaling: Evaluated effective magnification $\mu$ and systematic error budgets (lensing-vs.-merger kinematic broadening, $r_{J,1}$, and $\alpha_{\text{CO}}$ variations) in HARPAS-51 to scale the distance indicator to large SMG samples (Wu et al. + Birkin et al.) for joint maximum-likelihood constraints on $(\Omega_{m0}, w_0, w_a)$.
