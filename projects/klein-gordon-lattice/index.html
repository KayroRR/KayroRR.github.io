1. Physical Motivation & Continuum Limit

Classical and quantum field theories arise naturally as the continuum limit of discrete mechanical systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom. Consider a one-dimensional network of $N$ coupled oscillators separated by lattice spacing $a$, subject to nearest-neighbor harmonic coupling $\kappa$, an on-site pinning potential $m\omega_0^2$, and anharmonic self-interaction terms ($\lambda$):

$$L = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \left[ \frac{1}{2}m\dot{q}_n^2 - \frac{1}{2}\kappa(q_{n+1} - q_n)^2 - \frac{1}{2}m\omega_0^2 q_n^2 - \frac{\lambda}{4}q_n^4 \right]$$

Defining the spatial coordinate $x = na$, mass density $\rho = m/a$, tension $\tau = \kappa a$, wave speed $c = \sqrt{\tau/\rho}$, and rescaled scalar field $\phi(x,t) = \sqrt{\rho}\,q_n(t)$, taking the continuum limit $a \to 0$ ($N \to \infty$ with $L = Na$ fixed) transforms the discrete equations of motion into the relativistic Klein–Gordon field equation (with quartic self-interaction when $\lambda \neq 0$):

$$\left(\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} - \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + \mu^2\right)\phi(x,t) + \tilde{\lambda}\phi^3(x,t) = 0, \qquad \mu \equiv \frac{\omega_0}{c}$$

2. Numerical Methodology

  • Structure-Preserving Time Evolution: Implemented a second-order symplectic Velocity Verlet integrator to evolve the coupled Hamiltonian system over long integration windows without artificial numerical dissipation.
  • Discrete vs. Continuum Benchmarking: Initialized localized Gaussian wave packets and standing wave profiles across progressively finer lattices ($N = 32, 64, 128, 256, 512$), comparing the discrete trajectory $q_n(t)$ directly against the analytical Fourier solution of the continuum Klein–Gordon equation.
  • Fourier Mode Energy Transfer: Projected the instantaneous lattice state onto normal coordinates $Q_k(t)$ to track modal energy conservation in the linear regime ($\lambda = 0$) and nonlinear inter-mode energy cascade when anharmonic coupling ($\lambda > 0$) is activated.

3. Key Results

In the harmonic regime ($\lambda = 0$), the $L^2$ norm difference $\|\phi_{\text{discrete}}(t) - \phi_{\text{KG}}(t)\|_{L^2}$ exhibits second-order spatial convergence $\mathcal{O}(a^2)$ as the lattice spacing decreases, confirming the exact emergence of continuum Klein–Gordon dispersion $\omega^2(k) = c^2(k^2 + \mu^2)$ for long-wavelength modes ($ka \ll 1$). When anharmonic terms are introduced, high-wavenumber lattice dispersion competes with nonlinear steepening, driving structured energy transfer across Fourier modes while total Hamiltonian energy remains bounded to $\Delta H / H_0 \sim 10^{-10}$.
